# CT Power Washing Pro --- Risk Registry Development Notes

## Purpose

The purpose of this risk registry is to identify, evaluate, prioritize,
and mitigate risks that could affect CT Power Washing Pro's people,
customers, assets, operations, reputation, cash flow, and approximately
\$250,000 in annual profit.

## My Approach

I organized the assessment around:

1.  Employee and customer safety
2.  Property damage and service quality
3.  Vehicles and equipment
4.  Chemicals and environmental compliance
5.  Customers and reputation
6.  Financial and cash-flow risks
7.  Employees and key-person dependency
8.  Business continuity and seasonality
9.  Cybersecurity
10. Contracts and documentation

## Actions Taken

### 1. Identified major business risks

I considered activities and conditions that could create financial,
operational, legal, safety, or reputational consequences for a
power-washing company.

Key risks identified included employee injuries, customer injuries,
property damage, vehicle accidents, equipment failures, chemical
exposure, severe weather, customer complaints, employee turnover,
cash-flow problems, equipment theft, regulatory issues, pricing
pressure, and inadequate documentation.

### 2. Evaluated impact

Each risk was assigned an impact score from 1 to 5.

I considered potential:

-   Financial loss
-   Injury or safety consequences
-   Business interruption
-   Legal liability
-   Reputation damage
-   Customer loss
-   Effect on profitability

### 3. Evaluated probability

Each risk was assigned a probability score from 1 to 5.

The probability ratings are initial management estimates because
detailed historical company data was not available.

### 4. Calculated risk priority

The score was calculated using:

**Risk Score = Impact × Probability**

Scores were grouped as:

-   1--4 = Low
-   5--9 = Moderate
-   10--15 = High
-   16--25 = Critical

### 5. Developed mitigation actions

Mitigations were selected to reduce the likelihood of an event, reduce
its impact, or improve the company's ability to respond.

Examples include safety training, PPE, equipment maintenance,
before-and-after photographs, written contracts, insurance reviews, cash
reserves, weather monitoring, cybersecurity controls, employee
cross-training, and standard operating procedures.

### 6. Assigned risk owners

Each risk was assigned to a responsible role.

Primary roles include:

-   Owner
-   Operations Manager
-   Crew Lead
-   Fleet Manager
-   Equipment Manager
-   Office Manager
-   Bookkeeper
-   Sales Manager
-   Customer Service
-   IT Provider

The goal is accountability: every significant risk should have someone
responsible for monitoring and mitigation.

### 7. Added target due dates

Target dates were added to turn the register into an action-oriented
management document rather than a static list of risks.

Critical risks were given earlier target dates because of their higher
priority.

## Key Assumptions

The initial registry assumes:

-   CT Power Washing Pro is a small-to-medium-sized
    power-washing/exterior-cleaning business.
-   Annual profit is approximately \$250,000.
-   The company uses vehicles and trailers.
-   Employees or contractors perform exterior-cleaning work.
-   The company uses pressure-washing equipment and cleaning chemicals.
-   Weather and seasonal demand affect operations.
-   Detailed historical claims, accident, financial, insurance,
    employee, and regulatory data were not provided.

These assumptions should be validated and updated.

## Highest-Priority Risks

The initial assessment produced two Critical risks:

1.  Seasonality/revenue concentration --- score 20
2.  Severe weather --- score 16

Several risks received High priority scores of 15, including
employee/customer injury, property damage, vehicle accidents, chemical
exposure, cash-flow disruption, key-person dependency, and
pricing/margin erosion.

These scores are not permanent conclusions. They should be reassessed as
actual data becomes available.

## Data I Would Collect Next

To improve the accuracy of the risk register, I would collect:

-   Insurance policies and coverage limits
-   Workers' compensation claims
-   Vehicle accident history
-   Equipment repair and replacement history
-   Customer complaints and refunds
-   Monthly revenue
-   Revenue by service
-   Job-level profit margins
-   Employee turnover
-   Safety incidents and near-misses
-   Customer payment timing
-   Equipment replacement costs
-   Weather-related cancellations
-   Applicable environmental and wastewater requirements

## Next Actions

1.  Validate each risk with the business owner.
2.  Replace estimated probability ratings with historical data where
    possible.
3.  Confirm the appropriate risk owners.
4.  Confirm realistic due dates.
5.  Review insurance coverage with an insurance professional.
6.  Review applicable Connecticut and local requirements.
7.  Establish monthly monitoring for Critical risks.
8.  Establish quarterly reviews for High risks.
9.  Record mitigation completion dates.
10. Re-score risks after controls are implemented.

## Review Process

The register should be reviewed regularly and updated after:

-   A significant accident
-   An insurance claim
-   A customer property-damage incident
-   A major equipment failure
-   A regulatory change
-   A new service offering
-   A significant staffing change
-   A major change in revenue or operating model

## Personal Working Reflection

The main objective was to make the risk registry practical rather than
simply listing potential problems. I focused on connecting each risk to
a measurable score, a responsible owner, a mitigation action, and a
deadline.

The next improvement should be to move from estimated risks to
evidence-based ratings using CT Power Washing Pro's actual operating,
financial, safety, insurance, and customer data.
